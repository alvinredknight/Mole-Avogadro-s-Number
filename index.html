Simulator A The Mole

A mole is a counting unit, like a "dozen", but much, much bigger. Move the slider or tap a preset.

1 mole = 6.02 × 1023 atoms or molecules
6.02 × 1023 atoms or molecules = 1 mole
Each ornament = 1.00 × 1023 particles (a partly filled ornament is a fraction of that)

Part 1 6 points 0 / 6

Calculate the number of molecules (or atoms) from the number of moles, and the number of moles from the number of molecules (or atoms).

Guide: number of particles = moles × 6.02 × 1023  |  moles = particles ÷ 6.02 × 1023
Example 1: molecules in 2 mol H₂O → 2 × 6.02 × 1023 = 1.20 × 1024 molecules
Example 2: 3.01 × 1023 molecules of CO₂ → 3.01 × 1023 ÷ 6.02 × 1023 = 0.50 mol
Type large numbers like 1.2e24 or 1.2 x 10^24. Round to two decimal places.

Simulator B Molar Mass

The molecular mass of a compound is written in grams per mole (g/mol). It tells how many grams are in 1 mole. All molecular masses are rounded to two decimal places; use the values given.

⚖ 0.00 g
A full block = 1 mole = the molecular mass in grams. The grams in the blocks add up to the sample mass on the scale.

Part 2 6 points 0 / 6

Calculate the number of moles from a sample in grams, and the grams from a number of moles. Use the list of molecular masses below (rounded to two decimal places). Use these given values in all your calculations.

Guide: moles = grams ÷ molecular mass  |  grams = moles × molecular mass
Example 1: 36.04 g of H₂O → 36.04 ÷ 18.02 = 2.00 mol
Example 2: 0.5 mol of NaCl → 0.5 × 58.44 = 29.22 g
Round to two decimal places.
